For a Results section, keep it objective and save the interpretation for the Discussion. Here's a more elaborate version suitable for a manuscript.

> **Identification of recurrent differentially expressed genes across Parkinson's disease datasets**
>
> Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) identified from the three substantia nigra RNA-seq datasets (GSE133101, GSE136666, and GSE168496) were compared using a Venn diagram to evaluate the reproducibility of transcriptomic alterations across independent Parkinson's disease (PD) cohorts. DEGs were defined using an adjusted *P*-value < 0.05 and an absolute log2 fold change (|log2FC|) > 1.
>
> A total of 30, 24, and 109 dataset-specific DEGs were identified exclusively in GSE133101, GSE136666, and GSE168496, respectively (Figure X). Limited overlap was observed between individual datasets, with three DEGs shared between GSE133101 and GSE136666, two shared between GSE133101 and GSE168496, and nine shared between GSE136666 and GSE168496. Notably, no DEG was found to be significantly differentially expressed across all three datasets under the applied significance thresholds.
>
> The observed overlap pattern indicates that while each dataset captures distinct transcriptomic alterations associated with PD, only a small subset of genes exhibits reproducible differential expression between independent cohorts. The absence of universally shared DEGs likely reflects the substantial biological and technical heterogeneity inherent to human post-mortem transcriptomic studies, including differences in patient demographics, disease progression, tissue quality, sequencing protocols, and cohort composition. Consequently, although robust common DEGs were not identified across all three datasets, the pairwise overlapping genes represent recurrent candidates that may warrant further functional characterization.

A more detailed discussion of why no three-way overlap was observed should be reserved for the **Discussion** section, where you can cite literature on inter-study heterogeneity in Parkinson's disease transcriptomics.
